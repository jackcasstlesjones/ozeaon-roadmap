The proposal

Four releases · 46 days in total

The specification describes seven groups of notifications, delivered in four places: an unread count, a dropdown panel, a full notifications page, and a settings screen. Each release below is a complete, usable slice of the feature - nothing is left half-wired between them.

Recommendation

Ship the 12-day first release now. It delivers everything the platform can genuinely send today, on every surface.

Where we are starting from

Nothing in the product sends a notification today. There is an unused placeholder from the original build, but it cannot carry what the specification asks for - who did the thing, what it was about, which of your accounts it belongs to, or when you read it. So this is a build from scratch rather than an extension, which is what the first five days of the timeline reflect.

One thing already works in our favour: switching between your personal account and an organisation is live, and that is what lets a notification be addressed to an organisation rather than a person.

What has to wait, and why

Payments & funding

Just under half the list describes events the platform cannot yet produce, because the feature that would produce them has not been built. These are not cheaper to build later - they are not buildable now.

Group Days Waiting on
3D1-4 8 Payments. There is no payment provider connected yet, so there are no payouts or transactions to notify anyone about.
3F 8 Funding rounds. Projects carry a funding target and a total raised, but named rounds - "Spring 2026" - do not exist as something the platform can reference, and nothing yet updates the amount raised.
3G 8 Contributions, refunds and rewards. None of it is built.
C5 - Article reviews. The feature does not exist yet.
D9 - Role changes. Already flagged as blocked in the discovery document.

Those 24 days are the notifications work only. Connecting a payment provider, building funding rounds, and building contributions are separate projects with their own estimates - they are prerequisites, not line items here.

A few of these notifications are also time-based - "ends in three days" - which means the platform needs to be able to send something on a schedule rather than in response to an action. Nothing in the first release is time-based, which keeps that work out of it too.

Is 12 days realistic?

Yes - provided the deferrals below are agreed now rather than decided under pressure in the second week.

The risk sits almost entirely in the first five days. That block covers the groundwork, all three surfaces and seven notifications, and the dropdown panel and notifications page carry 29 specified behaviours between them - including three different ways to clear notifications and two different meanings of "mark all as read". Everything after day five depends on that block, so an overrun there moves the whole timeline.

What we are deliberately leaving out

  1. Quick actions. Invitations and join requests link through to the relevant screen instead of offering accept and decline in the notification itself. Priced separately at three days.
  2. Settings. The specification has every alert on by default, so launching with nothing to switch off is coherent. One thing still needs deciding - see below.
  3. Grouping. "Five people liked your post" arrives as five notifications at first, not one. Noisier, but nothing is broken, and switching grouping on later needs no rework.
  4. Automatic clearing of old notifications. The 30-day and one-year rules do not bite for 30 days and a year respectively. We build the groundwork now and add the clearing before the first ones age out.
  5. Instant delivery. The unread count refreshes on a short interval rather than the moment something happens. The specification does not ask for instant, and it costs noticeably more to run.

Open questions

Five for design
  • The designs are marked in progress. All four screens are drawn and the settings screen is detailed down to the wording, but this estimate assumes no further structural change. If the layouts move, the front-end figure moves with them.
  • Every notification type needs its own icon. The specification says design assigns them. Until that list exists the notification row cannot be finished - and the same row is used in both the dropdown and the page.
  • How long the dropdown is, and how you see more. The specification says "between 10 and 25, design sets the exact number", and leaves open whether you scroll or press a button for more. Those are two different builds.
  • What mobile offers. The notifications page has seven actions on desktop; design chooses which of them mobile gets, and how marking something as read works on a touch screen.
  • "Mark all as read" currently means two things. In the dropdown it clears only what is on screen; on the page it clears everything. Both are reasonable - we just need confirmation that the difference is intended.

Decisions needed before we start

Six items
  1. Confirm the designs are final, or accept the front-end estimate as provisional.
  2. Assign an icon to each notification type.
  3. Set the dropdown length, and whether seeing more is a scroll or a button.
  4. Choose which actions the notifications page offers on mobile.
  5. Decide the default for cross-account visibility - whether someone acting for an organisation sees that organisation's notifications alongside their own from day one. Settings are not in the first release, so whatever we pick is what everyone gets.
  6. Confirm the blocked groups - 24 of the 46 days - are deferred, and that payments, funding rounds and contributions are estimated as projects in their own right.
